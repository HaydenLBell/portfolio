About this project

Try enough versions of a strategy on the same historical data and some of them will look excellent purely by chance. A backtest on its own can't tell you whether you found an edge or fitted the noise, and the version that looks best is often the one that was fitted hardest.

This tool takes a strategy's backtest results and estimates how likely they are to hold up on data the strategy hasn't seen.

What it checks

  • Deflated Sharpe ratio: Adjusts a strategy's Sharpe ratio for how many variations were tried, so a high score found by brute force counts for less.
  • Probability of backtest overfitting: Splits the history into blocks, tries every combination of in-sample and out-of-sample halves, and measures how often the in-sample winner falls behind out of sample.
  • Walk-forward testing: Re-fits the strategy on a rolling window and tests it only on the data that comes after.
  • A clear verdict: One report per strategy: likely real, uncertain, or likely overfit, with the numbers behind it.

What I'm working on

Small samples are the hard case. With a few years of daily data the statistics get noisy, so the tool has to say when it can't tell, rather than give a confident answer it can't back up.
